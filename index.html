About

I don't just write content. I build the message behind it.

Most of my work has happened inside real client projects at ABCDMEDIA, writing for B2C brands that had very little in common with each other — a skincare line explaining an ingredient, a travel business selling a country most people had never considered visiting, a furniture brand trying to sound like more than a product catalogue. Moving between them taught me that the writing itself is the last step, not the first.

What comes before it is understanding who is reading, what they already believe, and what would actually make them stop scrolling. I write differently for a 22-year-old comparing sunscreens than I do for someone pricing a sofa, and that difference is the point. Every word is chosen to move someone from "scrolling past" to "reading" to "acting."

Structures I reach for

The frameworks behind the copy

AIDA
Attention → Interest → Desire → Action

My default for promotional and product-launch content, where the reader needs to be pulled from a scroll into a decision within a few lines.

PAS
Problem → Agitate → Solution

Used for pain-point-driven categories like skincare, where the reader already feels the problem and needs the discomfort named before the fix.

BAB
Before → After → Bridge

Built for transformation stories — a routine, a trip, a room — where showing the shift matters more than listing features.

4Ps
Promise → Picture → Proof → Push

Used to strengthen longer promotional copy that needs to earn belief, not just attention, before asking for action.

Storytelling structure
Hook → Context → Conflict → Insight → Resolution → CTA

My structure for travel and lifestyle content, where the destination or product needs to feel like part of a story, not a listing.

Feature → Benefit → Outcome
What it is → what it does → what changes

The base translation layer under almost everything else I write.

Contains Hyaluronic Acid becomes helps draw moisture into the skin, leaving it feeling hydrated and plump.
Range

One writer. Different voices.

Conversational
"The pimple left. The mark didn't."
Short, spoken, and built to sound like something a friend would actually say.
Educational
What niacinamide is actually doing to your skin barrier
Explains ingredients and mechanisms in plain language, without flattening the science.
Persuasive
"You've tried the routine. Have you tried the right one?"
Leads with the reader's problem, not the product's ingredient list.
Storytelling
"It wasn't on anyone's list. That's exactly why we went."
Builds curiosity before revealing the destination or product.
Promotional
Three days left, and the price won't hold.
Communicates urgency without leaning on exclamation points to do the work.
Travel
"What if your next trip wasn't just a vacation — but a story you'll keep telling?"
Sells the experience first; the itinerary comes second.

Case studies

Applying the system, project by project

Skincare & Beauty B2C · Product education & conversion
Objective

Turn technical ingredient information into content that a non-expert audience would actually read and trust.

Audience

Buyers who know they have a skincare concern — acne, dullness, dehydration — but don't know which ingredients solve it.

Challenge

Product descriptions were accurate but forgettable: full of ingredient names, empty of reasons to care.

Strategy & framework

Problem → Education → Solution → Benefit → CTA, applied across serums, face washes, sunscreens and Vitamin C products.

Hook

Dull skin doesn't need another filter. It needs the right routine.

Copy
Sunscreen isn't the step you skip when you're in a hurry. It's the one that decides whether the rest of your routine was worth it. Lightweight, no white cast, reapplies without ruining your makeup.
CTA

Find your shade

My role

Wrote and structured product-education copy and short-form social captions across the skincare range.

Travel & Tourism Baku · Thailand · Malaysia · Sri Lanka · Northern Pakistan
Objective

Sell destinations and tour packages without falling into generic "visit this beautiful place" copy.

Audience

Travellers choosing between several similar-looking packages, deciding mostly on feeling rather than itinerary detail.

Challenge

Destination content tends to sound interchangeable — the same adjectives applied to any country with a beach or a mountain.

Strategy & framework

Storytelling structure — hook, context, curiosity, resolution, CTA — built around a specific feeling per destination rather than a features list.

Hook

What if your next trip wasn't just a vacation — but a story you'll keep telling?

Copy
Northern Pakistan doesn't ask you to sightsee. It asks you to slow down — for the drive, the tea stops, the villages with no signal and no rush. Not every trip needs a checklist.
CTA

See the itinerary

My role

Wrote destination storytelling, package promotion copy and captions across five travel markets.

Furniture & E-commerce Product-to-lifestyle messaging
Objective

Move product listings away from specifications and toward the life the piece of furniture actually fits into.

Audience

Online shoppers comparing near-identical listings on price and photos, with little to differentiate the copy.

Challenge

Descriptions read like spec sheets — fabric type, dimensions — with nothing that made a shopper picture owning it.

Strategy & framework

Feature → Benefit → Lifestyle → Desire → CTA, selling comfort and daily use rather than materials alone.

Hook

Built for the evenings you'll spend actually living in your living room.

Copy
Durable fabric matters less than what you do on it. This one's built for feet up, long calls, and the nights nobody wants to leave the couch.
CTA

Shop the collection

My role

Wrote product-promotional and social copy translating specifications into lifestyle-led messaging.
